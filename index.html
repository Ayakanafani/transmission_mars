02 — The Red Planet

MARS EXPLORATION · PLANETARY SCIENCE

A World of Its Own

A frozen desert, two mysterious moons and traces of an ancient world. There is more to Mars than meets the eye.

Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun, located around 228 million kilometres away. Its distinctive red colour comes from iron-rich dust and rocks covering the surface. A day on Mars lasts approximately 24 hours and 37 minutes, while a Martian year stretches across 687 Earth days. The planet is home to Olympus Mons, the largest known volcano in the solar system, and Valles Marineris, an enormous canyon system. Mars also has two small moons: Phobos and Deimos. Evidence suggests that liquid water once flowed across its surface, raising one of the biggest questions in science: could life ever have existed here?
